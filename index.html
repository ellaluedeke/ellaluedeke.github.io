I study adversarial robustness and behavioral verification in LLM-based systems — how a model's reasoning breaks down under pressure, and how to build trust guarantees that don't just stack another probabilistic layer on top of the original problem. My work spans adversarial perturbation of AI coding agents, agentic-system safeguards, and applied machine learning, grounded in cybersecurity and formal methods.

I'm a senior at the University of North Florida (CS major, Leadership minor, Hicks Honors College), currently a research intern at Sandia National Laboratories. This October I begin an MPhil in Advanced Computer Science at Cambridge (Churchill College), on my way to a PhD in AI security and trustworthy systems.

Experience

Research Intern — Sandia National Laboratories2026

Working with Dr. Reed Milewicz on reasoning collapse in LLM coding agents: a four-condition study of how code complexity and adversarial perturbation degrade agent performance, using a three-class perturbation taxonomy against a Trilinos scientific-software testbed. Central finding: failure isn't gradual — error character shifts qualitatively as perturbation increases, and model confidence often doesn't drop even as correctness collapses.

Built an LLM-based approach to detect physics-student misconceptions from writing, fine-tuning and evaluating against prompting baselines. Independently wrote and secured a grant proposal for an automated feedback-generation pipeline for data structures instruction.
