I study how language models reason about code and how we can tell when they get it wrong.

My MPhil project looks at security patches. At Sandia, I studied how coding agents handle complex tasks and misleading inputs in existing codebases.

Research

When does a security patch actually fix the bug?

University of Cambridge · MPhil thesis in progress · Supervisor: Rob Mullins

A patch can pass the test that revealed a bug and still fail on a case that the test suite missed. My thesis explores how language models and formal methods can help distinguish a fix that generalizes from one that only works on the cases we already know about. I’m interested in what evidence we can use to judge a proposed fix, especially when tests leave part of the program’s behavior unchecked.

Where do coding agents get stuck in an existing codebase?

Sandia National Laboratories · Research internship with Reed Milewicz · Summer 2026

I built an evaluation pipeline to study coding agents on tasks involving existing software. I varied task complexity and the strength of misleading inputs under controlled conditions. I wanted to see which kinds of errors surfaced as the tasks got harder and whether the same kinds of errors appeared when the inputs were deliberately misleading.

I also coauthored a US-RSE paper on research software quality about the judgment researchers bring to assessing and maintaining AI-assisted code.

RAMP · Reading students’ explanations in physics

University of North Florida · Undergraduate research with Indika Kahanda

A student can arrive at the right answer while holding a misconception that only shows up in their written explanation. I designed and evaluated prompts that asked language models to identify those misconceptions in short physics responses. We compared model outputs with instructor annotations and examined how much guidance the models needed to interpret the responses.

Publication · Evaluation code and synthetic examples

InfraLearn · A simulator for teaching energy security

UNC Charlotte · NSF Research Experience for Undergraduates · 2025

I built a Python and Flask simulator modeled on the interface of a solar monitoring gateway. The simulator gave students a way to explore how a networked energy device behaves in a controlled setting. We designed the activities for learners without a computer science background and published the work in the CISSE journal.

Publication · Simulator code
